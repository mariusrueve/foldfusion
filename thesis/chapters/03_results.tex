\section{Evaluation Setup}
\label{sec:results-setup}
The pipeline was evaluated on a curated panel of \num{2370} AlphaFold models following the workflow detailed in Chapter~\ref{chap:methods}. \num{1840} of these targets ultimately produced at least one post-JAMDAscorer placement. The target list follows the stratified sampling scheme described in Section~\ref{sec:data-and-inputs}. For each \gls{uniprot} target the pipeline detects binding pockets with DoGSite3, retrieves donor conformers from the SIENA database, aligns and transplants ligands, and optionally refines the complex with JAMDAscorer, a pose-only optimizer that requires pre-positioned ligands and performs no docking search. All aggregates, plots, and tables in this chapter are generated by a reproducible reporting pipeline, with operational instructions recorded separately together with the configuration snapshot used for the run.

Operational parameters mirror the defaults documented in Chapter~\ref{chap:methods}. SIENA enforces an active-site identity threshold of \num{0.85} and retains a single alignment per detected pocket sorted by identity, backbone \gls{rmsd}, and all-atom \gls{rmsd}. JAMDAscorer refines every placement in its deterministic optimization mode. Transplant clash scores discard monoatomic ions by default, and Appendix~\ref{app:reproducibility} summarizes the resulting organic versus ionic counts used in the aggregate plots.

Table~\ref{tab:attrition-funnel} traces attrition across the dataset. All \num{2370} configured targets were attempted, and SIENA delivered at least one donor hit for \num{2115} targets of \num{2370} targets (\SI{89.2}{\percent} of those attempted). Completion records therefore appear for \num{2115} targets, reflecting cases where a donor could not supply ligands despite a full pipeline pass. The LigandExtractor and JAMDAscorer stages then delivered \num{6658} post-refinement placements. The per-target median is two placements, which forms the basis of the analyses below. DoGSite3 frequently identifies multiple pockets per target and SIENA donors often carry several ligands, so a single protein can contribute multiple retained placements.

\input{tables/03_results/table_attrition_funnel.tex}

Runtime diagnostics for the matched sample of \num{303} targets are summarized in Figure~\ref{fig:stage-runtime} (Appendix~\ref{app:reproducibility}). Median per-target wall times are \SI{0.94}{\second} for DoGSite3, \SI{0.45}{\second} for SIENA, and \SI{0.41}{\second} for JAMDAscorer, with an overall median of \SI{6.0}{\second} from start to finish. Each histogram draws on stage-specific instrumentation from the same subset: DoGSite3 logs exactly once per target, SIENA emits entries only when a donor is found, and JAMDAscorer records timings per successfully optimized placement. Panel-wise sample sizes therefore differ. Only SIENA operates under an explicit memory ceiling (\SI{25}{\giga\byte}), and the other stages stay well within default limits.

\section{Internal Pipeline Performance}
\label{sec:results-internal}
Table~\ref{tab:overall-summary} summarizes the scope of the run: \num{1766} distinct experimental donors contributed \num{6659} ligands for transplantation, of which \num{6658} remained after the JAMDAscorer clean-up pass. The near one-to-one retention highlights that the optimizer rarely discards placements outright while still relaxing numerous minor steric issues. In combination with the medians reported in Figure~\ref{fig:stage-runtime}, a typical target completes in roughly six seconds end to end.

\input{tables/03_results/table_overall_summary.tex}

Downstream utility is quantified by applying the lightweight quality thresholds introduced in Section~\ref{subsec:metrics} (Table~\ref{tab:internal-success}). These thresholds are evaluated post hoc in the reporting pipeline and do not alter the runtime outputs. A local \gls{rmsd} cut at \SI{1.0}{\angstrom} keeps \num{4991} placements (\SI{75.0}{\percent}) while retaining coverage for \num{1683} targets. Restricting \gls{tcs} to the organic subset and enforcing the 0.35 gate preserves \num{4867} placements (\SI{73.1}{\percent} of all outputs) across \num{1634} targets. Combining both criteria yields \num{3592} placements (\SI{54.0}{\percent}) that still span \num{1457} unique targets, illustrating that a moderate two-metric gate preserves breadth without inflating manual triage effort.

\subsection{Pipeline Scorecard}
\label{subsec:pipeline-scorecard}
The reproducible reporting pipeline produces a concise scorecard that supports the aggregate plots above. All \num{2370} AlphaFold targets were attempted, and donor alignments were recovered for \num{2115} (\SI{89.2}{\percent}), which marks the point where the pipeline produces transferable ligands (Table~\ref{tab:attrition-funnel} and companion spreadsheets).

Applying the two-metric analysis gate ($\text{Local RMSD} \leq \SI{1.0}{\angstrom}$ and $\text{TCS} \leq 0.35$) retains \num{3592} placements (\SI{54.0}{\percent}) across \num{1457} proteins, while single-metric cuts keep roughly three quarters of the placements (Table~\ref{tab:internal-success}). Post-JAMDAscorer medians remain at \SI{0.386}{\angstrom} local \gls{rmsd} and 0.263 \gls{tcs} with narrow \SI{95}{\percent} confidence intervals (Table~\ref{tab:global-quality-stats}). JAMDAscorer reduces clashes by a mean of 0.088 while leaving poses unchanged (median $\Delta$Local RMSD \SI{0.00}{\angstrom}), as captured in Table~\ref{tab:jamda-deltas}. Instrumented wall times confirm interactive execution with a \SI{6.0}{\second} median and \SI{14.0}{\second} 95th percentile per target (Figure~\ref{fig:stage-runtime}). Together, these scorecard items show that the pipeline delivers strong placement quality without sacrificing throughput. The full metric panels, intermediate tallies, and timing logs are bundled alongside this document as a supplementary data package.

\subsection{Global Quality Statistics}
\label{subsec:global-quality-stats}
Global summary statistics for the post-JAMDAscorer set are reported in Table~\ref{tab:global-quality-stats}. The median local \gls{rmsd} over all placements \emph{with defined values} is \SI{0.386}{\angstrom} (\SI{95}{\percent} \gls{ci} \SIrange{0.372}{0.396}{\angstrom}). Restricting to the \num{6488} organic placements \emph{with defined local \gls{rmsd}} leaves this median essentially unchanged at \SI{0.387}{\angstrom}, whereas the \num{125} monoatomic ions \emph{with defined local \gls{rmsd}} center at \SI{0.301}{\angstrom}. \gls{tcs} rows are shown for both the complete cohort and the organic subset; ions contribute \num{170} entries to the clash statistics (median 0.568 with a \SIrange{0.445}{0.626}{} \SI{95}{\percent} \gls{ci}). \gls{lev} exhibits the expected spread, with an overall median of \SI{2.41}{\angstrom} (\num{6004} placements) and a \SI{95}{\percent} \gls{ci} of \SIrange{2.37}{2.45}{\angstrom}, reflecting side-chain variability around otherwise consistent backbones. \gls{lev} values are available only when the donor reference SDF is present and the active-site alignment identity equals 1.0; \num{144} monoatomic ions have defined LEV under these conditions. Across the study we track \num{208} monoatomic ions in total; the per-metric Ns (\num{125} RMSD, \num{170} TCS, \num{144} LEV) reflect these availability filters. Every median and confidence interval in the table is computed from the $N$ printed in the corresponding row.

\section{Optimization Effects}
\label{sec:results-optimization}
The \texttt{JAMDAscorer} refinement stage primarily resolves residual clashes while preserving the transplanted geometry. Figure~\ref{fig:jamda} places most placements below the diagonal, indicating lower post-JAMDAscorer \gls{tcs}, and Table~\ref{tab:jamda-deltas} quantifies a mean reduction of 0.088 with an upper-quartile gain of 0.179 across the \num{6066} placements that yielded matched pre/post measurements. Local \gls{rmsd} shifts remain centered at \SI{0.00}{\angstrom} (mean \SI{-0.007}{\angstrom}), showing that the pose-only optimization acts as a conservative clean-up rather than a re-docking step. Paired $\Delta$\gls{tcs}/$\Delta$\gls{rmsd} distributions in Appendix~\ref{app:supp-figs} corroborate that improvements are concentrated in the clash-heavy tail.

\section{Ligand Composition}
\label{sec:results-ligand-composition}
The common ligand landscape is dominated by biologically ubiquitous cofactors and buffer components. Across the \num{6658} post-JAMDAscorer placements, \num{6488} ligands contain multiple atoms and therefore participate in \gls{tcs} reporting, whereas \num{170} monoatomic ions are tracked separately in Appendix~\ref{app:reproducibility}. The organic cohort covers the chemically informative species used in downstream analyses, while the ionic subset retains defined local \gls{rmsd}, \gls{tcs}, and \gls{lev} entries; we therefore handle their clash scores separately because single-atom donors collapse the contact surface. Aggregate \gls{tcs} statistics in this chapter default to the organic subset unless explicitly stated otherwise. Metal ions (Zn$^{2+}$, Mg$^{2+}$, Ca$^{2+}$), small anions (SO$_4^{2-}$, Cl$^{-}$), and polyols dominate the raw frequency counts that feed the top-20 plots, followed by nucleotide cofactors such as \gls{fad}, \gls{fmn}, and \gls{nad}. This mix reflects both biological prevalence and the availability of high-quality donor entries in the PDB, and it highlights where additional filtering or weighting may be needed when prioritizing ligands for experimental follow-up.

To emphasize chemically informative ligands, buffer species are removed once—specifically \gls{gol}, \gls{edo}, \gls{peg}* (covering \gls{peg}, PE3, PE4, and PE8 codes), and \gls{mpd}—before recomputing the histogram in Figure~\ref{fig:top20-nonbuffer}. The filtered bar chart draws on the same organic cohort as the tabulated statistics and exposes a cofactor-dominated core: \gls{fad}, \gls{fmn}, \gls{nad}, \gls{sam}/\gls{sah}, and \gls{plp} now occupy most of the top slots, followed by heme derivatives and metal chelators. These are the ligands most likely to be biologically informative if retained during downstream curation.

All statistics in Table~\ref{tab:ff-af-summary}, Table~\ref{tab:ff-af-success}, Figure~\ref{fig:ff-af-dist}, and Figure~\ref{fig:ff-af-corr} operate on this matched data.

\section{Failure Modes}
\label{sec:results-failure-modes}
Three dominant failure classes explain the residual gap. \textbf{Pocket-state mismatches} arise when AlphaFold predicts collapsed loops. These cases often clear the local \gls{rmsd} threshold yet exceed the \gls{tcs} limit used in the analysis (Table~\ref{tab:internal-success}). \textbf{Metal coordination discrepancies} manifest as elevated \gls{tcs} despite low \gls{rmsd} when side-chain rotamers differ from the donor geometry, particularly for Zn$^{2+}$ and Fe--S clusters highlighted in Figure~\ref{fig:top20-nonbuffer}. Finally, \textbf{alignment outliers} occur at the tail of the identity distribution (Figure~\ref{fig:identity-bins}), where SIENA finds only distant donors and the \gls{lev} field is typically undefined because the donor reference or identity~$=1.0$ condition is not met. Figures~\ref{fig:failure_modes_case_studies:success},~\ref{fig:failure_modes_case_studies:collapsed}, and~\ref{fig:failure_modes_case_studies:metal} (Appendix~\ref{app:supp-figs}) pair these signatures with the PyMOL renders, presenting the failure modes.

Figure~\ref{fig:failure_modes_case_studies:success} shows the \gls{nad} placement in UniProt~P00383 (donor NAP\_A\_157) with local \gls{rmsd} \SI{0.169}{\angstrom} and \gls{tcs}~0.15, exemplifying the clean successes retained by the joint gate. Figure~\ref{fig:failure_modes_case_studies:collapsed} illustrates the collapsed-loop failure (P80882, SF4\_A\_76; \gls{tcs}~0.935 despite local \gls{rmsd} \SI{0.202}{\angstrom}) and Figure~\ref{fig:failure_modes_case_studies:metal} shows the metal mis-coordination failure (P08306, CU\_B\_270; \gls{tcs}~1.07 with local \gls{rmsd} \SI{0.220}{\angstrom}) that motivate manual review. The scenes correspond to the second through fourth entries of the companion case-reference table supplied with the supplementary materials, and the rendered images are archived with the thesis figure assets for reproducible reuse.

\section{Ablations}
\label{sec:results-ablations}
The low pairwise correlations reported in Table~\ref{tab:cross-metric} reaffirm that local \gls{rmsd}, \gls{tcs}, and \gls{lev} capture complementary error modes, justifying the two-metric success criterion used above and suggesting that \gls{lev} remains valuable as a tertiary check when rank-ordering placements for visual inspection.

Identity threshold sweeps (Table~\ref{tab:identity-threshold-sweep} and Figure~\ref{fig:identity-threshold-sweep}) confirm that tightening the SIENA identity cut above the operational 0.85 threshold has only marginal payoff. Coverage drops from \num{6658} placements at 0.85 to \num{6044} at identity~1.00 (a \SI{9.2}{\percent} loss), yet the joint success rate rises only from \SI{54.0}{\percent} to \SI{55.1}{\percent} and median metrics shift by less than \SI{0.015}{}. Consequently, the study keeps the 0.85 default and does not explore looser thresholds, where biological divergence between donors and targets becomes substantial.

Excluding monoatomic ions, the default for clash scoring, has a pronounced visual impact on the \gls{tcs} distribution (Figure~\ref{fig:ion-exclusion}, Appendix~\ref{app:supp-figs}) but only a modest effect on overall success rates (Table~\ref{tab:ion-exclusion}). Reintroducing the \num{170} ions into the calculation lowers the \gls{tcs} pass fraction from \SI{73.7}{\percent} (organic only) to \SI{72.8}{\percent} while leaving local \gls{rmsd} statistics unchanged. The gap is driven by the long tail of high-scoring metals.
